What is it?

Ard Blocks is a free game, in the style of well-known block-building games, set in a world of villages, masjids and deserts. Children explore, gather materials, build, look after animals and farm. Along the way they keep the five daily prayers, learn Arabic words and phrases, and follow a story that ends with Umrah in Makkah.

  • Ages: written for children about 8 to 12, and enjoyed by teenagers and families.
  • Modes: Journey (the story), Explore (no quest, no damage) and Creative (unlimited building).
  • Where it runs: in a web browser on a computer, tablet or phone, and as a desktop app. No installation is needed to play in the browser.

Is it safe?

  • No adverts and no purchases. Nothing in the game asks for money.
  • No accounts. There is no sign-up, email address or password. We do not ask for your child's name or age.
  • Saves stay on the device. Worlds and settings are stored in the browser (or the desktop app) on the device being used. We do not receive them.
  • No tracking. The game contains no analytics or advertising code. The website host may keep ordinary server logs, as any website does.
  • Violence. Players meet wild animals and a few hostile creatures, and hunting animals for food is part of the game (with Bismillah, and not while in ihram). Villagers are friendly: arrows pass through people, and striking a villager is something the game treats as wrong and counts against the player's iman. In Explore, Creative and Classroom worlds nobody can be hurt, and in Family Mode players cannot hurt each other in any world.
  • No gambling, no chance-based rewards, no dark patterns.

Playing with other people

Multiplayer is optional. One player "opens the world to friends" and shares a six-character code; others type the code to join.

  • Players choose a nickname. We suggest first names or nicknames only, never a full name, school or address.
  • Devices connect to each other directly (peer to peer), using a free public matchmaking service to find each other. Because of how this technology works, the other players' devices can learn the network address of your device while you are connected. This is normal for online games, but it is the reason we recommend joining only people you know.
  • There are no voice chats, no links and no private messages. Text chat is visible to everyone in the world.

Family Mode

Family Mode makes online play safer for younger children. It is on by default and can be changed in Options (from the title screen or the Game Menu).

  • Typing is replaced by a short list of friendly Quick Chat phrases, such as "As-salamu alaykum", "Thank you" and "Let's build!". Nobody in the world can type their own messages.
  • A host who has Family Mode on only accepts Quick Chat phrases from the people who join, even if they are not using Family Mode themselves.
  • Family Mode is on by default. When a world is opened to friends, the host sees a reminder to share the code only with people they know.
Teachers: a "Classroom World" has student chat off by default and gives the host controls to pause the class, send a message, switch chat on or off and remove a player. There is a separate teacher pack with lesson plans, worksheets and a letter for parents.

Faith and learning

  • Villagers call the adhan at the five prayer times, and the player can make wudu and pray. Praying and keeping up prayers affects how the game feels, but nobody is ever punished by being locked out of the game.
  • Every item and animal shows its Arabic name, and a Words book collects what the child has found. There is also a list of everyday phrases.
  • The religious content was written to be simple and respectful. It has not yet been reviewed by a scholar or a qualified teacher, so please read it with your own family's practice in mind, and tell us about anything that should be corrected.

Healthy play

  • A game day lasts about 40 minutes, and the prayer clock gives natural moments to stop and pray.
  • Autosave runs every minute, so it is safe to stop at any time. Use Save and Quit to Title to be certain.
  • Settings let you lower the graphics quality on older tablets and turn sounds and music off.

Your child's data

  • Where is it? Only on the device, in the browser's storage for this site (or the desktop app's own storage).
  • Make a backup: Game Menu → Export Backup saves a file you can keep or move to another device.
  • Delete everything: clear this site's data in the browser's settings (or uninstall the desktop app). Because nothing is stored on a server, that removes all of it.
